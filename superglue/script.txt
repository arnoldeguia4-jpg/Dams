Why is Super Glue so strong? Because it was rejected... twice.
In nineteen forty-two, during World War Two, a young chemist named Harry Coover was trying to make clear plastic gun sights for soldiers.
He tested a chemical called cyanoacrylate. The problem? It stuck to everything. His tools. His glass. Probably his fingers. Too sticky. Rejected.
Nine years later, his team tried it again, this time for jet airplane canopies. And again, it glued everything together.
But this time, Coover stopped and thought: wait. Maybe sticky isn't the problem. Maybe sticky is the product.
In nineteen fifty-eight, it hit store shelves. Just one drop could bond things in seconds.
That's why Super Glue is so strong. It was a failure, until someone realized the failure was the point.

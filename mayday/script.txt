Why do pilots yell "Mayday" when they're in trouble? Blame the French.
In nineteen twenty-three, London's Croydon Airport had a problem. Pilots called for help with S.O.S. But over a crackly radio, a single letter gets lost in the static. And a mistake could be deadly.
So, the story goes, a radio officer named Frederick Mockford was asked to find a better word. One that anyone could hear, and anyone could say.
Most of his flights went to Paris. And in French, "help me" is "m'aidez."
Say it fast, and it sounds like... Mayday.
By nineteen twenty-seven, it was the official distress call for the whole world. Pilots say it three times, so nobody can miss it.
That's why pilots yell Mayday. It's French for "help me," spelled the English way.

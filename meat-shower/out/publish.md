## YouTube description
On March 3, 1876, it rained meat in Kentucky. 🥩

Near Olympia Springs in Bath County, Mrs. Crouch was outside making soap under a clear sky when chunks of fresh red meat began falling around her. Soon there were hundreds of them, scattered over an area roughly the size of a football field (about 100 × 50 yards). Two curious neighbours decided to taste it: one said it was mutton, the other said venison. Scientists who examined samples couldn't agree either. Suggestions ranged from lung tissue to a jelly-like algae called nostoc.

The best explanation, still the leading theory today: a flock of vultures. Vultures often regurgitate their last meal when they're startled, so a whole flock "lightening the load" at once could explain the meat. Which means those two neighbours probably had lunch straight from a vulture's stomach.

⚠️ The vulture theory is the most popular explanation, but it isn't proven, and some witnesses said the sky was clear. A preserved sample is still kept at the Bath County History Museum.

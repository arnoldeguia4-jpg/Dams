Why is the Statue of Liberty green? Because she wasn't always.
In eighteen eighty-six, France gave America a giant gift: a lady made of shiny copper. She glowed like a brand-new penny.
But copper has a secret. When it meets rain, wind and salty sea air, it slowly changes.
Within about twenty years, Lady Liberty went from penny brown to minty green.
People panicked. In nineteen-oh-six, Congress set aside money to paint her back to copper.
New Yorkers were furious. Some called it sacrilege.
So engineers took a closer look, and found something surprising.
That green coat wasn't damage. It was armor. A thin skin called patina that protects the metal underneath.
So the paint was never used.
That's why the Statue of Liberty is green. Her "rust" is what protects her.

## YouTube description
In 1859, the United States and Great Britain almost went to war… over a pig. 🐖

San Juan Island sat between American and British territory, and both nations claimed it. On June 15, 1859, an American farmer named Lyman Cutlar found a Hudson's Bay Company pig eating his potatoes, and shot it. He offered $10; the British wanted $100 and threatened to arrest him. The Americans sent 64 soldiers under Captain George Pickett. The British answered with warships. By late August, 461 Americans with 14 cannons faced five British warships carrying 70 guns and over 2,000 men.

Nobody fired. Rear Admiral Robert Baynes refused to "involve two great nations in a war over a squabble about a pig." Instead, both sides kept small garrisons on the island for twelve years — and got along well enough to share holidays and social events. In 1872, Kaiser Wilhelm I of Germany, acting as arbitrator, awarded the islands to the United States.

The only casualty of the Pig War? The pig.

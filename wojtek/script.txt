In World War Two, one of Poland's soldiers was a bear. An actual bear.
Iran, nineteen forty-two. Polish soldiers met a boy carrying a tiny bear cub. They traded for it and named him Wojtek.
They raised him like a little brother. He drank milk from a vodka bottle, wrestled the troops, and slept curled up next to them at night.
There was just one problem. The army didn't allow pets.
So they made him a soldier. Private Wojtek got his own rank, his own rations and his own serial number.
Then came Monte Cassino. The soldiers swore he copied them, carrying heavy crates of artillery shells up to the guns.
They were so proud, they promoted him to corporal and put him on their badge. A bear holding a shell.
After the war, Wojtek retired to a zoo in Scotland.
And when his old comrades visited, they climbed right into his enclosure to wrestle him.

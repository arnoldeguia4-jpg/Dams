## YouTube description
In 1807, Napoleon Bonaparte, the man who conquered half of Europe, was chased off the field… by rabbits. 🐇

After signing the Treaties of Tilsit with Russia in July 1807, Napoleon wanted to celebrate with a rabbit hunt. His chief of staff, Marshal Louis-Alexandre Berthier, organized everything: hunters, guns, a picnic, and hundreds of rabbits. But they reportedly weren't wild rabbits. They were tame ones, bought from local farmers. So when the cages opened, the hungry rabbits didn't run away. They ran straight at the Emperor, apparently expecting to be fed. Whips couldn't stop them. Napoleon retreated to his carriage… and, the story goes, the rabbits followed him in.

⚠️ How true is it? The story comes from the memoirs of General Paul Thiébault, published in 1895, decades after the event. Thiébault reportedly disliked Berthier, and some numbers you'll see online (like "3,000 rabbits") aren't in reliable sources. Treat it as a famous anecdote, not settled history.

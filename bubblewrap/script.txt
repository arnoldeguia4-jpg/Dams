Why do we wrap things in bubble wrap? Because it failed... twice.
In nineteen fifty-seven, two engineers in New Jersey, Alfred Fielding and Marc Chavannes, had a wild idea: wallpaper you could touch.
They sealed two shower curtains together, trapping rows of tiny air bubbles. Bumpy, shiny, futuristic. And nobody wanted it on their walls.
So they tried again. Greenhouse insulation! Keep the plants warm with bubbles. That flopped too.
But then, in nineteen sixty, IBM had a problem. It was shipping its brand-new computer, and it needed something soft enough to keep it from breaking.
Those failed bubbles were perfect.
Today, bubble wrap protects almost everything we ship. And it gave the whole world a new hobby: pop, pop, pop.
That's why we wrap things in bubble wrap. It was supposed to hang on your walls.

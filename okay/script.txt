Why do we say OK? Blame a newspaper joke.
In eighteen thirty-nine, Boston editors had a strange hobby: abbreviating words wrong, on purpose.
"Enough said" became N.S., for "nuff said." "All right" became O.W., for "oll wright."
Then, on March twenty-third, the Boston Morning Post printed O.K., for "oll korrect." A misspelled "all correct."
It was a silly joke. It should have died with all the others.
But one year later, President Martin Van Buren ran for re-election. His nickname? Old Kinderhook. O.K.!
His fans formed O.K. Clubs and shouted it at every rally.
Van Buren lost the election. But the word won.
That's why we say OK. It started as a spelling mistake, made on purpose.

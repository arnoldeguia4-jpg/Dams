Why are school buses yellow? Blame one stubborn professor.
In nineteen thirty-nine, America's school buses were a mess. Some were red. Some were blue. Some were just farm trucks with benches in the back.
So Frank Cyr, a professor at Columbia University, called a big meeting in New York, with experts from all over the country.
He hung fifty strips of paint on the wall, from lemon yellow to deep orange-red. And they argued.
Finally, they picked one shade: a bright yellow with a hint of orange.
Why that one? Because your eyes catch it fast, even at dawn, even in fog, even out of the corner of your eye. And black letters pop right off it.
State by state, the buses turned yellow.
That's why school buses are yellow. One meeting picked the color, and it has protected kids ever since.

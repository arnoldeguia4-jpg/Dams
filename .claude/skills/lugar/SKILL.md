# LUGAR: user's premise → story + twist → Tagalog voice → 3D blocky short with word-by-word captions

LUGAR is the fourth sister of PALDO / PALDONT / WANGBU, but with its own look:
- **Characters:** real 3D **blocky Roblox-like rigs** (`kit/web/blocky.js`): rounded-cube head with a drawn face (16 expressions), blocky hair styles, box torso, limbs on joints, white shoes. Poses: idle, walk, run, wave, cheer, point, shrug, think, facepalm, hold, pray, sit, fall, jump, bow, dance.
- **World:** soft-lit pastel barangay (`kit/web/props.js`): road with dashes, pastel houses, sari-sari stalls with signs, trees, lamp posts, jeepneys, rooms, beds, phones, cups, money. Light presets `lugar` (bright day), `lugarDusk`, `lugarNight`. **No ink outlines** (soft PBR shading, like the reference).
- **Captions:** **one word at a time**, lowercase with its punctuation, **Nunito Black**, white with a thick dark rim and soft shadow, centred around y ≈ 1450 of 1920, each word popping in (86 % → 100 % in 70 ms) exactly when it is spoken. Generated by `kit/captions.py` → `out/captions.ass`, burned in by `finish.sh`.
- **Pill labels:** rounded pills in 3D space above people/things (`pill()` in `cast.js`): yellow ("10 TASA!"), red ("MALING JEEP!"), green ("✓ TAMA!"), white name tags ("KAPITAN!"), plus big `shout()` text, money rain, confetti, bursts, dizzy stars.
- **Language:** Tagalog / Taglish narration, first person ("ako"), playful and fast. Captions, pills and signs in the same language.

## Who does what
- **The user gives the topic** (a premise, a "what if", a funny situation: e.g. "isang barangay kung saan binabayaran ang pagkakamali").
- **You write the story and the ending.** Your job is the hook, the escalation and the **twist** that makes people stay to the last second. Don't change the user's premise; build on it.
- If the user's message has only a topic, run the whole pipeline. Show the script and the twist in your reply, with one line naming the other twist you considered, and continue without waiting unless the user writes "/lugar pili" or "ako pipili".

## How to talk to the user
Reply in Tagalog/Taglish. Short status lines per stage. Use AskUserQuestion only for real choices. "Tapos na ba?" → check the real frame count. Tell them they can close the app but must not archive the session.

## Rules
- **Style inspiration only.** Never recreate the reference video or any creator's video: no copying its story, character names (e.g. "Tonyo"), places ("Opisina ng Mali"), lines or shots. Make new characters, names and situations every time.
- No real living people, brands or logos. Generic stores, phones, apps.
- **Captions are part of the style**: always generate them (from the final voice), never skip. They must match the words actually spoken.
- Never edit or re-time the voice. The picture and captions follow the voice.
- Mix with `mix.py`: music exactly −6 dB vs voice; SFX ≤ −6 dB, peaks ≤ 0.7×. `finish.sh` re-limits if AAC overshoots −1 dBFS.
- Commit and push after major steps (`.gitignore` already covers frames, stills, logs, wav).

## Stage 1: Premise
Restate the user's premise in one line. If it is very short, invent the world rules that make it funny (who, where, what's the absurd rule).

## Stage 2: Story + twist (the heart of LUGAR)
Shape (first person, Tagalog):
1. **Hook (first 3 s):** the absurd rule or a shocking statement, said as normal life. ("Sa barangay namin, kapag nagkamali ka… ikaw ang babayaran.")
2. **World:** 2–3 quick examples of the rule, each a mini-gag with a pill label (counts, prices, names).
3. **The narrator's problem:** they are the exception (too good / too unlucky / the only one who…). Make the audience root for them.
4. **Escalation:** 3 attempts that each backfire more, with one big visual joke.
5. **Lowest point:** alone, at night, "bakit ako?" moment.
6. **TWIST:** a reversal that uses something planted earlier (a number, an object, a throwaway line), so the end feels earned, not random. Good twist types: the flaw is the superpower; the rule flips; the narrator was the cause all along; the "villain" was helping; the whole thing was a test/contest; a time skip shows the real payoff; the last line reveals who's really telling the story.
7. **Button:** one last line or a final pill/shout that lands the joke (≤ 6 words).
Plant at least two details early that the twist pays off. Keep sentences short (captions are one word at a time).
**Word budget:** start at ~2.2 words/s for Tagalog (measure after the first take and update this file): ≈ 130 words → 60 s, ≈ 190 words → 90 s.
Reply with the script in a code block (one phrase per line), word count, estimated length, the twist in one line, the planted details, and a shot plan. Save it as `script.txt`.

## Stage 3: Voiceover (ElevenLabs)
`creative_create_flow` → `creative_generate_speech` (model `eleven_v4`, the chosen Filipino voice, `estimate_only` first, then 2 takes) → poll `creative_get_flow_run_status` → curl the takes into `takes/`. Measure with ffprobe + silencedetect, send both, use the closer one as `voice.mp3`.

## Stage 4: Timing + captions
- `mkdir -p work out && python3 timing.py voice.mp3 script.txt --out work` → `work/phrases.json`; frames = round(duration × 30).
- `python3 captions.py work/phrases.json out/captions.ass` → word-by-word ASS. For exact word timing, transcribe the voice with `creative_transcribe_audio` (word timestamps) and save `work/words.json` as `[{word,start,end},…]`, then pass `--words work/words.json`. Check a few words against silencedetect gaps.

## Stage 5: Build the animation (see `references/lugar-api.md`)
1. `cp -r .claude/skills/lugar/kit/* <slug>/` (includes `fonts/Nunito-Black.ttf`, also copied into `web/` for signs and pills).
2. Rewrite `web/story.js`: `setup()` builds the sets (props.js) and the rigs (`makeBlocky(spec)`), `frameAt(f, H, R)` poses every rig with `pose(rig, f, {pos, yaw, pose, t, expr, look, lean, armL, armR})`, hides the ones not in the shot, moves props, and returns fx cards (pills, shouts, bursts, money rain). SHOTS hold the cameras; sets for other places go far away on x.
3. Camera language from the reference: mostly eye-level medium shots and close-ups on faces (the face is the joke), quick cuts every 1–3 s, a push-in on each reaction, white flash on place/time jumps, pills popping above heads on the key word.
4. Stills: `node render.mjs stills/a "<mid-frame of every shot>"` → `python3 sheet.py stills/a stills/sheet_a.jpg`; fix framing (faces not cut, pills inside the frame, captions zone y 1350–1550 kept clear of important action), re-render, then send the sheet.

## Stage 6: Sound
`audio.py` (playful score + SFX cue table at frames/30): set `STOP` at the twist reveal, add cues for every pill pop (pop), money (coins/ting), falls (thud/boing), phone notifications (ting), crowd (applause). Run it, then `mix.py`.

## Stage 7: Render, finish, deliver
Two workers `RESUME=1 nohup node render.mjs frames "0-<N/2>"` / `"<N/2+1>-<N-1>"` (≈ 2.5–4 s/frame each). Monitor. Then `bash finish.sh "Title"` (burns `out/captions.ass`, loudness, QA, `out/qa_sheet.jpg`). Check one frame for the caption, send the MP4, short Tagalog summary, commit and push.

## Stage 8: Thumbnail
Add a `thumb` shot at frame 9000 (hero close-up with a big expression + the key object/pill + money rain or glow). `node render.mjs stills/thumb "9000"`, edit the text in `thumb.py` (white question/setup + yellow payoff, Nunito Black), `python3 thumb.py`, check, send both.

## Stage 9: Publishing pack (see `references/publish.md`)
Title (Tagalog hook + emoji), long description, short caption, YouTube tags (phrases, ≤ 500 characters total) and hashtags; also offer the word-by-word tag list if the user likes that format. Save as `out/publish.md`.
